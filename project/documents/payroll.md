# Payroll & Compensation Knowledge Base

## Overview

This document provides guidelines and procedures related to employee payroll, compensation structure, tax declarations, and stock-based compensation benefits.

The purpose is to help employees understand salary components, payment schedules, tax compliance requirements, and compensation-related processes.

---

# 1. Pay-Day Schedule

## Purpose

To define the monthly payroll processing and salary payment timeline.

---

## Payroll Cycle

The company processes payroll on a monthly basis.

Typical payroll activities include:

1. Attendance and leave data validation.
2. Variable pay and deduction calculation.
3. Payroll processing.
4. Salary approval.
5. Salary credit to employee bank accounts.

---

## Salary Payment Date

Employees receive salary on the scheduled payroll date communicated by HR.

Example:

- Payroll Period: 1st – 31st of every month
- Salary Credit Date: Last working day or predefined payroll date

---

## Payroll Delays

Employees should contact Payroll if:

- Salary is not credited on the expected date.
- Salary amount differs from expected value.
- Incorrect deductions appear.

---

## Payroll Support Information

Employees should provide:

- Employee ID
- Payroll month
- Issue description
- Supporting documents (if required)

---

# 2. Tax Declaration Process

## Purpose

To collect employee tax-saving declarations and ensure accurate tax deductions.

---

## Tax Declaration Period

Employees are required to submit investment declarations during the financial year as communicated by HR/Payroll.

---

## Declaration Process

1. Log in to the employee payroll portal.
2. Navigate to **Tax Declaration** section.
3. Enter eligible investments and deductions.
4. Upload supporting documents if required.
5. Submit declaration before the deadline.
6. Payroll team reviews submitted information.

---

## Common Tax-Saving Declarations

Employees may declare:

- Provident Fund contributions
- Insurance premiums
- Eligible investments
- Home loan details
- Education-related deductions
- Other government-approved deductions

---

## Proof Submission

Employees must:

- Submit valid documents.
- Ensure information is accurate.
- Meet submission deadlines.

Failure to submit proofs may result in higher tax deductions.

---

# 3. Salary Structure

## Purpose

To explain the components that make up employee compensation.

---

## Components of Salary

A typical salary structure may include:

### Fixed Compensation

Includes:

- Basic Salary
- House Rent Allowance (HRA)
- Special Allowance
- Other fixed allowances

---

### Statutory Benefits

Includes:

- Provident Fund (PF)
- Professional Tax (where applicable)
- Insurance contributions
- Other legally required deductions

---

### Variable Compensation

May include:

- Performance bonus
- Incentives
- Project-based rewards
- Annual variable pay

---

## Salary Components Explanation

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Basic Salary | Core salary component used for statutory calculations |
| HRA | Housing-related allowance |
| Allowances | Additional fixed benefits |
| Variable Pay | Performance-based compensation |
| Deductions | Statutory and employee contributions |

---

## Salary Revision Process

Salary revisions may depend on:

- Performance review results
- Market benchmarks
- Business conditions
- Promotion or role changes

---

# 4. Stock Option Vesting

## Purpose

To explain employee ownership benefits through stock options.

---

## Stock Option Overview

Stock options provide eligible employees the opportunity to purchase company shares at a predefined price after meeting vesting conditions.

---

## Vesting Schedule

A typical vesting schedule may include:

- Initial grant date
- Vesting period
- Periodic vesting milestones
- Completion of vesting conditions

Example:

| Period | Vesting Status |
|--------|----------------|
| Grant Date | Options awarded |
| First Year | Initial vesting milestone |
| Following Years | Remaining options vest periodically |

---

## Vesting Conditions

Employees may need to:

- Remain employed with the company.
- Complete required service period.
- Meet applicable company conditions.

---

## Exercising Stock Options

After options vest, employees may:

1. Review available vested options.
2. Decide whether to exercise options.
3. Complete required approvals.
4. Pay exercise price (if applicable).
5. Receive shares according to company rules.

---

## Leaving the Company

Employees should review:

- Vested option status.
- Exercise deadlines.
- Expiration rules.
- Company stock plan terms.
